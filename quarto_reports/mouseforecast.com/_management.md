+ Monitor mouse activity in crops before harvest, particularly in WA's Geraldton and Esperance regions, SA's Mid North and Yorke Peninsula, the Wimmera, South East NSW and South East Queensland, and decide early whether baiting is needed.

+ Monitor using chew cards or a walk-through checking for crop damage (https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management/monitor-manage/know-your-mouse-numbers). 
Active burrow counts are generally less reliable at this time of year.

+ If you find mice or damage, consider applying zinc phosphide bait (https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management/monitor-manage/mouse-baiting) before crops mature. 
Bait becomes less attractive once grain develops in the head, so baiting ripening crops is unlikely to be effective.

+ Growers sowing summer crops should check paddocks and neighbouring stubble before planting, and consider baiting at or immediately after sowing if mice are present.

+ ZnP25 is registered nationally. Use it according to the label and report any adverse effects via the APVMA website (https://www.apvma.gov.au/regulation/adverse-experience-reporting-program). 
An emergency permit currently allows use of the higher-dose ZnP50 bait. Check where it applies, its conditions and its expiry on the GRDC website (https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management).

+ Aim for a clean harvest with minimal grain loss to reduce food for mice in stubble, and keep monitoring after harvest, as mouse numbers then set the risk for next season's sowing.
