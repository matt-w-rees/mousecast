+ Monitor mouse activity in crops before harvest. This is particularly important in locations shaded red in @fig-chance below, as these areas have a greater than 50% chance of moderate or high mouse numbers.

+ Monitor using chew cards or a walk-through checking for crop damage ([GRDC: know your mouse numbers](https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management/monitor-manage/know-your-mouse-numbers)). 
Active burrow counts are generally less reliable at this time of year due to poor visibility. 

+ Aim for a clean harvest with minimal grain loss to reduce food for mice in stubble, particularly where a wet finish delays harvest. Keep monitoring after harvest, as mouse numbers then set the risk for next season's sowing.

+ If you find mice or damage after harvest, consider applying zinc phosphide bait ([GRDC: mouse baiting](https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management/monitor-manage/mouse-baiting)) before sowing. 
Most winter crops are now close to maturity, and bait becomes less attractive once grain develops in the head, so in-crop baiting is only worthwhile in crops still well short of maturity (be aware of the 14-day withholding period specified on the label).

+ Growers sowing summer crops should check paddocks and neighbouring stubble before planting, and consider baiting at or immediately after sowing if mice are present.

+ ZnP25 is registered nationally. Use it according to the label and report any adverse effects via the [APVMA adverse experience reporting program](https://www.apvma.gov.au/regulation/adverse-experience-reporting-program). 
An emergency permit currently allows use of the higher-dose ZnP50 bait. Check where it applies, its conditions and its expiry on the [GRDC mouse management page](https://grdc.com.au/resources-and-publications/resources/mouse-management).
