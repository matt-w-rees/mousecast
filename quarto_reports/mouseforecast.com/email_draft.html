Mouse Forecast

June 2026 update

Hi all,

A new Mouse Forecast is now available, and some changes to the way we communicate these updates.

We have launched a new website - mouseforecast.com - where you can see the full report with the most up-to-date information.

We will regularly update the website as new data comes in (and continue to send these emails at key times of the growing year). A pdf copy is also attached to this email, feel free to forward on to your relevant network along with the website URL.

Mouse activity estimates are now completely based on quantitative data. Live-trap, active burrow, chew card assessments are integrated with citizen reports via MouseAlert. We have kept the same “traffic light” colour coding, but shifted to a continous scale to show detail. We are actively refining statistical methods to improve estimates, and improve their presentation. Again, we welcome any feedback you may have.

Overview

  • Western Australia: A mouse plague is under way across multiple regions. The worst outbreak is occurring in the “Northern” agro-ecological zone (Geraldton port zone), followed by another east from Fitzgerald in the “Sandplain” zone (Esperance port zone). Mouse activity should be monitored through winter. As crops start to mature, consider baiting before grain fill, as zinc phosphide becomes less effective once grain develops in the head. Mouse abundance in WA “Eastern” and “Central” zones appears to be low, but growers here should remain vigilant.

  • South Australia: There is consistent moderate mouse activity across Adelaide Plains and the Yorke Peninsula. We have observed a slight decrease in activity from last season, likely due to lower food availability and proactive management during sowing. However, mouse activity could increase as more food becomes available. Continue monitoring mice as crops mature, and be ready to act before grain fill if activity increases.

  • Elsewhere: mouse numbers likely remain low. Although there is low-moderate activity across the NSW NE Qld SE zone.

  • An emergency permit has been issued by the APVMA for the higher dose ZnP50 bait: see GRDC website for details.
