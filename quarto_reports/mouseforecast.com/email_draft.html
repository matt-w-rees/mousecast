Mouse Forecast

Spring 2026 update

Hi all,

A new Mouse Forecast is available, along with some changes to the way we communicate these updates.

We have launched a new website - www.mouseforecast.com - where you can see the full report with the most up-to-date information.

We will regularly refresh the website as new data comes in (and continue to send these emails at key times of the growing year, the next due in Spring).

A pdf version of the forecast is also attached to this email, please forward on to your relevant network.

Mouse activity estimates are now completely based on quantitative data. Live-trapping, active burrow, and chew card assessments are integrated with farmer observations via MouseAlert. We have kept the same “traffic light” colour coding but shifted to a continuous scale to show detail. We are actively refining statistical methods to improve these forecasts and their presentation. Please let us know if you have any feedback.

Overview

  • Overall:

    • The plague in WA’s Northern (Geraldton) region is likely to continue.
    • Elsewhere, surveys have found mostly low mouse activity so far, but conditions have been outstanding in the south, with strong crop growth and, across much of SA and the Victorian Mallee, a recent dry-to-wet rainfall swing. In the north, good winter growth has been followed by a dry spring, which should ease the risk there if it continues.
    • The forecast points to moderate numbers in places (notably the Victorian Mallee, parts of the WA Sandplain, northern NSW and southern Queensland) and high numbers on SA’s Far West and Upper Eyre Peninsula, although that is very uncertain.
    • If populations build while these conditions last, plague risk could rise in the seasons ahead, so we have some cause for concern.

  • Western Australia:

    • Northern (Geraldton): the plague has slowed but not crashed. Recent rainfall has favoured mice, and plagues can sometimes run into a second year.
    • Sandplain (Esperance): moderate in about a third of the region after exceptional winter crop growth.
    • Central and Eastern: mostly low.

  • South Australia:

    • A recent dry-to-wet rainfall swing, which tends to precede plagues, points to high numbers on the Far West and Upper Eyre Peninsula and around Jamestown. This is very uncertain, as few recent surveys exist there.
    • Mostly low elsewhere, but the rainfall swing means any area where numbers reach moderate in future seasons could quickly progress to a plague.

  • Victoria: Moderate in the Mallee north of Birchip (towards Sea Lake and Swan Hill) after excellent winter crop growth; with the recent rainfall swing, these populations could progress to a plague, so we are watching closely. Low elsewhere.

  • New South Wales: Mostly low. Good winter growth raised the chance of a build-up (about even in the north, around Moree and Gunnedah; one in three elsewhere), but the dry spring and low survey results so far make this less likely.

  • Queensland: Mostly low. Good winter growth raised the chance of a build-up to about even on the Darling Downs and along the border (around Dalby and Goondiwindi), lower around St George, but the dry spring and low survey results so far make this less likely. The forecast is least reliable here.
