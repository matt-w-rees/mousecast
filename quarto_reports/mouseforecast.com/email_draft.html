Mouse Forecast

Spring 2026 update

Hi all,

A new Mouse Forecast is available for the remainder of Spring on www.mouseforecast.com.

Excitingly, we are now using an updated statistical model, allowing a more accurate forecast at a finer spatial resolution. This model better considers recent environmental conditions and crop growth alongside mouse survey data. Please let me know if you have any feedback on how the forecast is presented.

A few more Spring mouse surveys are due to roll in, and we will refresh the website afterwards (and continue to send these emails at key times of the growing year).

The next round of mouse surveys will occur over summer in QLD and northern NSW. For more southern regions and Western Australia, our next surveys are scheduled from March 2027. Due to recent favourable conditions, we are particularly interested in your observations of mice in paddocks post-harvest - please log these on MouseAlert.

A pdf version of the forecast is also attached to this email, please forward on to your relevant network.

Cheers, Matt.

Overview

  • Western Australia:
    • Northern (Geraldton) region: The mouse plague has slowed, but recent spring surveys confirm mouse numbers remain high (likely favoured by recent rainfall).
    • Sandplain (Esperance) region: Mouse numbers are expected to be moderate across more than half of the region, particularly around Esperance and Salmon Gums, following exceptional winter crop growth.
    • Central and Eastern regions: Mouse numbers are likely to remain mostly low.
  • South Australia:
    • Surveys have found mostly low numbers so far, except on southern Yorke Peninsula, where surveys detected moderate numbers.
    • However, above-average winter crop growth and a recent swing from long-term dry to wet conditions (which often comes before outbreaks) point to a risk of higher numbers in coming seasons.
    • With no recent surveys in the Far West, Upper Eyre Peninsula or around Jamestown, the model forecasts high numbers there from conditions alone — but with considerable uncertainty.
  • Victoria:
    • Mallee: Mouse numbers are potentially moderate in parts of the Mallee north of Birchip, towards Swan Hill. This is likely due to excellent winter crop growth, and a recent swing from long-term dry to wet conditions increases the risk of numbers rising further in coming seasons.
    • Elsewhere: Mouse numbers are expected to be low.
  • New South Wales:
    • Mouse numbers are mostly low, except for possible moderate numbers in parts of the Liverpool Plains (near Gunnedah) and the eastern Riverina (near Temora and Wagga Wagga).
  • Southern Queensland:
    • Mouse numbers are expected to be mostly low, but with patches of moderate activity (particularly around St George, although there are no recent surveys there to confirm this).

Most likely level of mouse numbers in Spring 2026 predicted across key grain-growing areas (shown at a 10 × 10 km spatial resolution). “Low” numbers reflect typical yearly fluctuations in mouse numbers, with “moderate” numbers rising above normal levels where crop damage is possible (potentially in a build-up phase), and “high” approaching or at concerning numbers where crop damage is likely. The forecast is based on past mouse surveys, environmental conditions in the previous season, and this season’s surveys at nearby paddocks.
