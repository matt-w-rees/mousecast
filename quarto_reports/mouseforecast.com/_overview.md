+ [Western Australia]{.underline}:
  - Northern (Geraldton) region: The mouse plague has slowed, but recent spring surveys confirm mouse numbers remain high (likely favoured by recent rainfall).
  - Sandplain (Esperance) region: Mouse numbers are expected to be moderate across more than half of the region, particularly around Esperance and Salmon Gums, following exceptional winter crop growth.
  - Central and Eastern regions: Mouse numbers are likely to remain mostly low.

+ [South Australia]{.underline}:
  - Surveys have found mostly low numbers so far, except on southern Yorke Peninsula, where surveys detected moderate numbers.
  - However, above-average winter crop growth and a recent swing from long-term dry to wet conditions (which often comes before outbreaks) point to a risk of higher numbers in coming seasons.
  - With no recent surveys in the Far West, Upper Eyre Peninsula or around Jamestown, the model forecasts high numbers there from conditions alone --- *but with considerable uncertainty*.

+ [Victoria]{.underline}:
  - Mallee: Mouse numbers are potentially moderate in parts of the Mallee north of Birchip, towards Swan Hill. This is likely due to excellent winter crop growth, and a recent swing from long-term dry to wet conditions increases the risk of numbers rising further in coming seasons.
  - Elsewhere: Mouse numbers are expected to be low.

+ [New South Wales]{.underline}:
  - Mouse numbers are mostly low, except for possible moderate numbers in parts of the Liverpool Plains (near Gunnedah) and the eastern Riverina (near Temora and Wagga Wagga).

+ [Southern Queensland]{.underline}:
  - Mouse numbers are expected to be mostly low, but with patches of moderate activity (particularly around St George, although there are no recent surveys there to confirm this).
